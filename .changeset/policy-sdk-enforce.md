The SDK owns the policy deploy flow, so every surface runs the same one. `aomi.account.policy.enforce(agentWalletId, entry, signer, options)` prepares the step, checks the signer and the action (the connected account is the policy's owner, for every kind of action; the action's network, a MetaMask target, no value attached), has the wallet act and confirms, retrying while the chain settles. It answers `{ policies, settled, transaction? }`: `transaction` is the Solana signature or EVM hash the wallet returned, and `settled` is false, not an error, when the backend's node still does not show a sent transaction after the retries. `AomiPolicySigner.sendEvmTransaction` may answer the hash. A step that fails after a transaction was sent throws `AomiPolicySentError`, which carries `transaction` and the `cause`; a signer throws it too when what it sent reverts, and `aomi policy deploy`/`undeploy --json` and the widget read the reference from it. A Solana transaction's signature is passed to the confirm step, which records it. `AomiPolicySigner` names the connected accounts as `evmAddress` and `solanaAddress`; a signer that leaves out the one the policy's owner needs is refused before the wallet is asked. A surface passes an `AomiPolicySigner`: sign EIP-712 data, send an EVM transaction, send a Solana transaction. `signableTypedData`, `policyFromDraft`, `upsertPolicy`, `toBaseUnits`, and `enforcementKey` are exported; the widget calls them and keeps only what it shows. In the widget, a step that asks nothing of a wallet needs none connected once the policy's owner is known, a sent transaction that Aomi does not see yet is named by its hash, and a Solana transaction is sent only when the wallet's selected network is the cluster it names.

Breaking: `aomi.account.rules` is `aomi.account.policy`, with no alias. The types follow: `AomiRule*`/`AomiRules*` are `AomiPolicy*` (`AomiRuleChange` is `AomiPolicyChange`, `AomiWalletRules` is `AomiWalletPolicies`, `AccountRulesTransport` is `AccountPolicyTransport`), `RULES_ERROR_CODES` is `POLICY_ERROR_CODES`, `hasRules` is `hasPolicies` and `ruleKey` is `policyKey`. Wire field names and error code strings still say `rules`; the REST paths are `/policy` (see `policy-paths-next`).
