`aomi policy` replaces `aomi rules`. Breaking for CLI users: `aomi rules` is removed with no alias. `aomi policy list` prints each agent wallet's mode and authority, then a table of its policies with where each stands on chain: `in sync (<provider>)`, `not deployed`, `out of date (<provider>)`, `not verified (<provider>)`, `on chain only`, `needs whitelist mode` or `Aomi only`. A policy that is on chain while its provider still asks the owner for something (`next` is not null, such as a MetaMask revocation still to send) reads `out of date`, not `in sync`; `policyOnChain` decides it. `aomi policy catalog [--chain <c>]` lists the apps and tokens a policy can name. `aomi policy set <json | path>` or `aomi policy set --amt <amount> <asset> --to <app-id|address> [--until YYYY-MM-DD] [--chain <c>]` adds or replaces one policy; `remove <policy-id>`, `mode whitelist|blacklist` and `authority self|<address>` change the rest; `approve` and `decline` keep their behaviour, and refuse a change that belongs to another wallet than `--wallet`. Every write prints what changed, whether it LOOSENS or TIGHTENS, whether it is applied or waits (the server's answer decides, not the rows shown), and a `Next` block with the command that brings the chain in line; that command names `--wallet` when the wallet was named. A write left waiting for an approval or a signature exits 3. `--yes` also approves a change when the authority is Aomi-managed. Every command takes `--wallet <id>` and `--json`.

`aomi policy deploy <policy-id> [--provider <p>]` puts a policy on chain and `aomi policy undeploy <policy-id>` takes it off, with the local signer (`aomi wallets signer set`, `--private-key` or `--solana-private-key`) through `aomi.account.policy.enforce`. Both show the provider, the signer and the action first, ask `Sign and send? [y/N]` (`--yes` skips it), then print prepare, sign + send (with the sent transaction's signature or hash) and confirm and the resulting on-chain status of that provider. When Aomi's node does not show a sent transaction in time they still print it, say to check again before sending anything more, and `--json` carries `settled: false` and `transaction`. An EVM transaction is sent with the key the command connected with, and its hash is kept when the receipt does not come in time. An empty `<policy-id>` is a usage error and `matchPolicyIds` matches nothing for it. They refuse a policy Aomi no longer holds (deploy), one no provider can hold, and a local signer that is not the wallet that owns the policy; with no local key for the owner they print where to do it and exit 3. `undeploy` also works on a policy already removed from Aomi while a chain still holds it.

Client: `aomi.account.policy.revise(agentWalletId, build)` reads the policies, builds the change from them and writes it, once more when they moved in between (`rules_changed`). `checkPolicy` validates a policy against the catalog and `policyFromInput` builds one from what a person types (a symbol, an app id or address, a day); both throw `AomiPolicyFieldError`, which names the field and the nearest catalog apps. `policyFromInput` reads `to` as an app id first, then as a label among the apps on the token's chain. `policyOnChain` and `chainOnlyPolicies` say where a policy stands on chain, and `expectedPolicyAction` what its provider will ask the owner's wallet. `catalogOn`, `withoutPolicy`, `fromBaseUnits`, `assetChain` and `assetNetwork` are exported; the widget now takes the last three from the client.

A policy with no on-chain entry, in a wallet in blacklist mode, on a chain where Swig or MetaMask is offered, reads `needs whitelist mode`, not `Aomi only`: those providers hold policies in whitelist mode only. `Next` then names `aomi policy mode whitelist`, and the portal's policy row says the same under `Onchain Enforcement: None`. The SDK decides it: `policyOnChain` answers `{ kind: "needs_whitelist", provider }`, and `whitelistOnlyProvider` is exported. `aomi policy deploy` refuses, before it asks and with exit 3, a local key that would own the policy but is not one of the account's wallets; a prepare step refused with `account_not_found` prints the same guidance. `aomi whoami` marks the key that did not sign in as `local signer only`. `aomi policy deploy` and `undeploy --json` print `{ "error": { "message", "code"?, "transaction" } }` on stdout when a step fails after a transaction was sent. After a sent EVM transaction, `enforce` stays `settled: false` while the entry still asks for that transaction (`next` unchanged), not only while a signed revocation waits.
